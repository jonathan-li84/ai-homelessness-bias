Media Coverage and AI Estimates of Homelessness

This dashboard compares blind AI estimates with HUD's January 2024 Point-in-Time counts, Census population estimates, and full-year GDELT homelessness-headline location mentions. The analysis measures association, not causation.

Research question

How much additional variation in the accuracy of AI homelessness estimates is associated with local media coverage across California CoCs, after accounting for population and actual homelessness levels?

Main result

+0.0023Change in R²
p = 0.695Incremental F-test

The population-and-PIT model had an R² of 0.4079. Adding article count raised it to 0.4102, so media coverage provided almost no additional explanatory value for AI absolute percentage error. If article count truly added no linear explanatory value, an F-statistic at least as large as the observed one would occur in 69.5% of repeated samples. The increase therefore provides no convincing evidence that article count improves the model.

Data sources

  • U.S. Department of Housing and Urban Development: FY2024 California CoC boundaries, January 2024 PIT and HIC counts, and CoC Performance Profiles.
  • U.S. Census Bureau: 2020–2024 ACS five-year population estimates from table B01003 and 2024 TIGER/Line census-tract boundaries.
  • Blind AI estimates: One estimate of each CoC's 2024 PIT total, recorded before comparison with HUD's reported count.
  • GDELT: English homelessness-related Article List headlines from January 1 through December 31, 2024, joined to GKG California location annotations and spatially assigned to CoCs.

Limitations

GDELT identifies every California location mention, not necessarily the main location of an article's homelessness discussion. Secondary comparisons can therefore count for a CoC, ambiguous place names may be assigned incorrectly, and syndicated stories may appear under multiple URLs. GDELT coordinates are fixed reference points for named places, which is why many articles share identical latitude and longitude.

The analysis includes only 44 CoCs and uses one blind AI estimate per CoC. Population and PIT counts are strongly related, which increases uncertainty. The regression describes an adjusted association and cannot show that media coverage caused the AI estimates to become more or less accurate.
