Research question

How do the accuracy and consistency of AI homelessness estimates vary across California CoCs?

Fifty successful Gemini API runs are compared with January 2024 HUD PIT counts. The study examines where estimates are more accurate, where they vary more across requests, and whether those patterns are related.

2024 PIT benchmark44 California CoCs50 runs per CoC2,200 estimates
What is compared
January 2024 HUD PIT totals and the model’s estimates for the same 44 Continuums of Care, not individual cities or counties.
Two separate questions
Accuracy measures the difference from PIT. Consistency measures how much the 50 responses differ from one another.
Scope of the findings
These results describe the retained responses. Geographic differences alone do not establish what caused the errors.

Study design, definitions & data

Understand the measures, check the study’s limitations, and access the data and figures behind the dashboard.

06 / STUDY

How to read the measures

Accuracy
Each estimate is compared with its CoC’s January 2024 HUD PIT total. Absolute errors measure the size of the difference, while signed errors retain overestimation or underestimation.
Consistency
Gini’s mean difference averages the absolute differences between all 1,225 distinct pairs among 50 estimates. Relative GMD divides that value by actual PIT and multiplies by 100.
Ranges and averages
Ranges show the observed minimum and maximum, not confidence intervals. Each CoC is one geographic observation.
Repeated requests
The first 50 successful Gemini API requests provide 50 estimates for each of 44 CoCs. There are 2,200 responses, but 44 geographic observations in the correlations and regression.
How the main measures are calculated

Signed error (people) = AI estimate − actual PIT. Signed error % = signed error ÷ actual PIT × 100. Absolute errors remove the sign before averaging across responses.

GMD (people) = the average absolute difference across the 1,225 distinct pairs among 50 estimates. Relative GMD (%) = GMD ÷ actual PIT × 100. Relative estimate range (%) = (maximum estimate − minimum estimate) ÷ actual PIT × 100.

Data sources

  • U.S. Department of Housing and Urban Development: FY2024 California CoC boundaries, January 2024 PIT and HIC counts, and CoC Performance Profiles.
  • U.S. Census Bureau: 2020–2024 ACS five-year population estimates from table B01003 and 2024 TIGER/Line census-tract boundaries.
  • Repeated AI estimates: First 50 successful Gemini API runs (gemini-3.8-flash as identified in the first batch log), totaling 2,200 estimates. Failed requests and the final six successful requests were excluded. Exact API prompt, settings and dates were not supplied.
  • GDELT: English homelessness-related Article List headlines from January 1 through December 31, 2024, joined to GKG California location annotations and spatially assigned to CoCs.

Limitations

GDELT identifies every California location mention, not necessarily the main location of an article's homelessness discussion. Secondary comparisons can therefore count for a CoC, ambiguous place names may be assigned incorrectly, and syndicated stories may appear under multiple URLs. GDELT coordinates are fixed reference points for named places, which is why many articles share identical latitude and longitude.

Repeated responses are not 2,200 independent geographic observations. Ranges describe the observed responses and are not confidence intervals. PIT is a one-night estimate with measurement limitations. The exploratory regression uses 50-run mean absolute percentage errors and cannot establish causation.
